## What the baseline addition resolves

The three fixed references are genuine human-label-trained predictors, rather than evaluation oracles. The specification retains all three, separates 877 training people from 556 target people, uses person-level folds, and reports prediction and contrast outcomes without choosing a favourable reference. Prior human inspection is acknowledged. This is a meaningful improvement over the earlier absence of a practical statistical reference.

The public evaluator now exposes the two group components and their separate missing-action bounds as well as moderation. That directly addresses the cancellation problem identified earlier. The continued distinction between conditional-mean predictions and respondent trajectories is appropriate. None of these additions is, by itself, methodological novelty.

## What the calculated results establish—and do not

According to the aggregate report, absolute moderation error is about **55.16 CAD** for both the training-mean and recipient-only references, versus **65.46 CAD** for interacted OLS. Thus a constant can score closer on moderation while reproducing neither group's recipient-income responsiveness. This is a concrete source-specific illustration of endpoint interpretation, now obtained from disjoint-human prediction rather than an invented response table.

However, the ranking difference is only about **10.30 CAD**, without a justified importance threshold. It is not yet a consequential failure of the research conclusion. Nor does the interacted model become the more faithful respondent simply because it represents income interactions: recipient-only OLS has lower author-reported main MAE/RMSE (**844/1,050 CAD**, versus **849/1,053**) and slightly lower out-of-fold errors too. The components have different error patterns. There is no universal ordering of these models across the reported objectives.

The interacted reference's target components reproduce its training-group coefficients because its predictions lie in the same fully interacted linear span and no clipping occurred. That agreement with the remainder's coefficients is an algebraic transport property of the fitted predictor, not an independent validation of those component values in the main humans. The earlier main/remainder sensitivity therefore remains central.

These results do not establish that respondent income is inherently uninformative, that LLMs will add value, or that a label-free model should beat a human-trained predictor. The reference family is deliberately simple; training labels, permitted features and output interpretation differ from the planned model arms. Conversely, that difference does not make the references useless: they now provide a transparent practical point of comparison. Preserve the stated information accounting and report all three rather than seeking another baseline until a preferred ranking appears.

## Remaining nontrivial question and redesign

The closest prior work already combines socioeconomic-structure assessment, income counterfactuals and income omission, as recorded in the earlier review of [arXiv:2609.33055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33055). Changing the outcome to welfare allocation and adding a standard regression comparison does not resolve that overlap. Full-v2-text access remained unverified in that earlier review; no stronger access claim is made here.

A defensible core question is now: **Which matched welfare-allocation inferences are supported by the generated responses, and does adding the observed fact bundle move the recorded contrast closer to the human reference without that description concealing its component errors?** Explicit-income omission remains the secondary controlled comparison. This reframes the scientific claim around what an investigator can conclude, while preserving J_FN, J_FI and the locked cohort.

The potential increment is a real, diagnosed disagreement between a synthetic-respondent conclusion and the matched human reference, or a useful bounded case of agreement, situated against the trained references and stronger same-output checks. It cannot merely be that a difference score omits information about its components; that is known mathematics. A narrow application/validation study remains possible. A broad methods paper would need evidence that the diagnostic lesson has practical or transferable value beyond this selected cohort.

## Minimum next step and insufficient outcomes

Freeze one reporting table containing both components, moderation, signed errors, J_FN/J_FI, reference prediction errors, invalid-action ranges and the existing diagnostics. State the distinct interpretations prospectively; do not replace the primary endpoint with a post hoc composite or introduce a preferred human subset. Keep the corrected bundle-versus-explicit-income distinction.

Then finish the declared isolated execution, model/settings/cost, repeat and presentation plans, and obtain actual N/F/F-I outputs. The useful empirical question is whether those checks change or qualify a concrete conclusion on those outputs. Another oracle, baseline family or code expansion is not the next scientific requirement.

A tiny J improvement, a fragile moderation-sign reversal, recovery of the obvious recipient-income ordering, or beating the constant alone would remain insufficient evidence of useful respondent simulation or novelty. If all that results is a small source-specific score change with no important diagnostic or practical implication, the honest contribution is a limited domain replication. The present baseline results improve evaluation credibility; they do not yet justify lifting the generation hold or claiming that the larger scientific contribution has been secured.
