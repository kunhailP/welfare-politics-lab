DECISION
Use Bridgman Study 2 as the human anchor, but do not submit a bare replication or a generic LLM deservingness audit. Build a Track C evaluation of whether synthetic citizens recover context-dependent redistribution rules. This is the current strongest feasible option, not a proven winning entry.

Working title
Matching the Mean, Missing Redistribution? Human-Grounded Tests of Synthetic Welfare Politics
This is a question. No model result has been generated, and neither failure nor success is predetermined.

Substantive question
When government assistance changes from an ordinary tax rebate to crisis relief, do synthetic citizens reproduce humans' changes in the weight placed on recipient income, citizenship, employment and other randomized characteristics? Recovering the overall spending level is distinct from recovering the distributional rule.

MINIMUM COMPLETE EXPERIMENT
A. Human baseline: reproduce published-code descriptive and attribute effects, including respondent-level dependence; compare core coefficients with published figures. Sensitivity to exclusions, raw CAD vs frame-mean normalization. Do not erase null or inconsistent attribute effects.
B. Actual synthetic responses: use original single-profile, blank-dollar-input task and all original randomized attributes. Preserve four tasks per respondent agent and policy frame. Use population-level Canadian citizen role unless pre-treatment persona variables are verified. Study 2 does NOT contain the Study 1 respondent demographic columns. Do not construct ideological personas from get_ahead/inequality/living or government support items until their measurement timing is verified.
C. Comparator: constant-within-frame software control; it can match frame means by construction but has zero attribute effects. Label access to human means as an oracle diagnostic, not a successful out-of-sample predictor. Include a simple train-only attribute regression comparator if a predictive split is used. LLMs must add value beyond trivial baselines.
D. Core scores: raw allocation distribution and means; normalized attribute-effect errors; primary low-income-by-frame effect difference; citizenship and employment effects as prespecified secondary endpoints; invalid/refused/extreme response rates. Do not convert a non-significant human effect into a hard zero or score models solely by sign agreement.
E. Controlled extension: compare isolated-task versus preceding-task-history input on the SAME profiles and model; this estimates sensitivity of the synthetic measurement procedure. Existing human data do not randomize the memory mechanism, so differences are not human memory effects. This is optional and follows, rather than replaces, the human anchor.
F. Source/target calibration if affordable: fit any scale/prompt adjustment only on a declared source-frame development subset, freeze it, then evaluate the other frame. Reciprocal direction is a robustness exercise. No tuning using target outcomes, published target coefficients, or unpublished model results. Because this design was developed after examining human aggregate results, describe it as an analysis plan locked before new model generation, not a preregistered human replication or untouched external validation.
G. Decision rule: report recovery or failure regardless of direction. If model effects do not outperform baselines within uncertainty, narrow claims and explain the deployment limitation. Do not search variants until a dramatic positive or negative result appears.
